# Research playbook: employer context is not candidate work

1. Verify **identity of employer** (name, domain, headquarters, acquisition and duplicate names). Confirm the candidate's business unit and location where possible.
2. Anchor every finding to the actual job date range. Current marketing copy is not proof that a 2018 employee worked on a product released in 2025.
3. Prefer official annual reports, product documentation, dated case studies, developer blogs, conference talks, public job descriptions and credible third-party reporting.
4. Distinguish `official capability`, `public case study`, `probable workload`, and `candidate work`. Only the last is eligible for experience bullets.
5. Build a hypothesis matrix (employer, relevant product/system theme, dated source, technical constraints, candidate verification question, confidence).
6. Research model/framework/service release dates when they determine chronology. Make no assumptions about beta availability, private previews or internal inventions.
7. Capture source URL, publication date and observation date; for current claims refresh sources.
8. Identify a primary engineering narrative per role without manufacturing project assignments. The career should show progression in architecture and ownership.
9. Preserve uncertainty explicitly: "Public sources show X; candidate participation unconfirmed."

## Research output

- Verified employer identity and business model.
- Two or three period-appropriate engineering problem spaces, labeled hypotheses.
- Dates and citations for each external fact.
- Five to eight targeted follow-up questions that can confirm real implementations.
- Explicit statement of what the research **does not** prove.

Do not copy customer case studies into experience bullets, even if the user asks for "variations." A fictional example may be used for interview preparation, unmistakably marked example-only.
